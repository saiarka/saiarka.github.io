DevLog 1(0?)

First off, hello. This is my first blog post on this site. I'm barely half a beer in and I've been tinkering and toying with a game design idea that I realize now is some kind of unsolvable puzzle for me, and so here I am documenting it. For context, I've been 'working' on this game for around 3 (?) months. I'm giving myself grace knowing I've been waffling about making jack shit all progress on this thing, but whatever I'm sticking to that number. 3 months of work and what have I got to show for it? Two squares. Oh, sorry, red squares. Brilliant. It was a fantastic idea creating a whole game from scratch. I mean sure I have almost nothing to show for it, but uhhhhh I'm a better programmer???? Still unclear on that too.

Anyway, the real point of this/these post(s) is maybe an effort to document my development process and encourage a line of dialogue with myself. I am a scatter-brained individual by nature. My thoughts veer into unexpected corners at the slightest digression. This is a good place to 'let it all out' so to speak. If someone decides to read this, firstly apologies for the terrible writing, and secondly apologies for making a half-assed game. I say this assuming that people would only bother to read it if I had made a game worth looking into. Then some lone personage in his digital curiosity might decide to browse through my repository and find this sad excuse of a blog among my carcasses of failed projects. Yes I'm a highly confident person how did you know?

Sorry, gamedev, right. Let's get back on track. I'm having a lot of trouble with this 'richochet game' idea. I just can't seem to crack it. Maybe it's because I'm shooting too high in my ambitions as a noobie game designer, or maybe it's just cause there was really nothing there to begin with regarding the idea of the game. Simplicity was the goal. In my head I thought I would take the satisfying visuals of Bullseye's pinpoint, comically divine accuracy and implement it in a game. Guy shoots a projectile, and you as the player get the chance to direct the richochet of the ball at every some n odd hits. Honestly, tooting my own horn here a little, I think it's not a bad idea. Different types of projectiles have different trajectories. Certain walls could influence gravity or velocity. A lot of possibilities there. The only issue I'm having is how I would even begin to design a level. What would the player do beyond launching the projectile? What would the end goal be? What's stopping this projectile from getting to the goal? As I ruminate on these questions I just can't find anything inspiring or interesting to answer back. It's gotten to the point that I'm now considering scrapping this idea and going with something new; I mean I just started making the game so I would really be losing only the game design progress. I've realized recently that I am actually quite a stubborn person, and that if I don't force myself to exit from an idea I've cornered myself into I'll never get out.

Question is, what's the new game going to be? I still want to keep it very simple in terms of feature set and overall 'design'. I want it to be 2-D. I like some of the aesthetic notes I made earlier while developing out this richochet idea. If I'm speaking (or writing I guess) frankly, my attraction towards a game is entirely based on the artstyle, ambiance, and feel of the gameplay. A game like Signalis may not be appealing to me on a gameplay level, but I've been consistently enamoured with the soundtrack and visuals. A game like Celeste may not touch my aesthetic fancy, but the simplicity of design and the cleanliness of its overall execution are highly attractive. Not sure I'm going to be able to come up with an idea tonight, but I like what I've written so far. Maybe I'll use it as a guiding point.
